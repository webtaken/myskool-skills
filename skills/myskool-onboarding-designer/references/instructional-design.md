# Instructional design for a Skool community — the rules

The design method in this file is adapted from the Creator Skool
community-building system. The same rules are enforced by MySkool's
blueprint linter (`validate_blueprint` → `lint`), so a design that follows
this file validates clean.

A member cancels when the first month ends without a result they can name.
Every rule below exists to get them a visible result early and show them the
next step before they finish the current one.

## 1. Pick one engine

A community has one dominant reason members come back. Decide it first; the
classroom, the calls and the posts all follow from it.

| The member today… | Engine | Why they come back | Blueprint `engine` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knows what to do but can't sustain it | **Challenges** (daily habit, streaks) | They don't want to break the streak | `challenges` |
| Already executes and gets stuck on details | **Live calls** (audits, hot seats) | The expert corrects their work | `live` |
| Starts from zero | **Step-by-step program** (guided practice) | They want to unlock the next phase | `theory` |

No community is pure: challenges need a little theory, a program needs a
call. Aim for the dominant engine to be 60–80 % of what happens. If the
creator has under 2 hours a week for members, the engine cannot be live
calls: propose challenges with one closing call a week.

## 2. Promise and transformation

- **State A** in the member's own words (what they say when they complain).
- **State B** measurable: a number, a milestone or a sustained action. If it
  can't be measured, rewrite it.
- **Promise** = result + for whom + by when, 20 words at most. No "transform
  your life", "unlock your potential".
- The method gets a name and 3–4 phases. Members get an identity (what they
  call themselves).

## 3. Three acts of a member's life

| Act | When | What must happen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · The call | week 1 → month 1 | A **first visible win within 7 days**, and a result they feel **before day 14** |
| 2 · The trials | months 2–3 | Practice, obstacles, corrections. Most cancellations happen here: this is where re-entry challenges and audits go |
| 3 · The return | month 3 on | The result, a new identity, and **the next arc already in sight** (season challenge, higher tier, guide role) |

Write the concrete win of each act for this community.

Why day 14: in monthly subscriptions, whoever feels no value in the first two
weeks rarely renews — between 39 % and 58 % of subscribers are lost at the
first renewal (RevenueCat, 2026).

## 4. Classroom rules

1. **Module 0 "Start here"** (`Empieza aquí`): what this is, how to use it,
   and the first task to do today. Always open to everyone.
2. **First win in week 1.** The first task takes under 20 minutes and ends in
   something the member can post.
3. **Lessons of 3–7 minutes, one task per lesson.** A long recording is a
   masterclass or a call replay, not a lesson. Short units with one action are
   finished far more often than long ones.
4. **Every task starts with a verb you can see done**: publish, write, upload,
   record, send. Never "reflect on", "think about", "consider".
5. **Every module has a deliverable** the member can show in the feed.
6. **Name modules after their result**, 2–5 words. Never "Module 1:
   Introduction".
7. **Unlock the main path by days since joining, never by level.** Skool
   levels rise with likes received (level 9 needs 33,015 points), so gating
   essentials by level punishes the member who practices quietly. Levels are
   for bonuses only.
8. Order modules by the three acts, not by loose topics.

Shape by engine:

- **Challenges** — few short modules: one per challenge with each day's step,
  plus a tools module.
- **Live calls** — a library of recordings **by problem** ("nobody buys",
  "I don't know what to post"), not by date, plus "how to ask for your audit".
- **Program** — phases with short lessons, one task each and one deliverable
  per phase. Run it as **cohorts with a start date**: fixed-schedule courses
  are completed more often than self-paced ones (53.4 % against 41.9 % across
  1.3 million enrollments; Ruzuku, 2026), and open self-paced courses sit
  around 3 % (Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente, *Science*, 2019). Propose a cohort
  that starts every month.

## 5. Challenges are a ladder, not a single event

A lone challenge ends and the member leaves. A ladder always shows the next step.

| Type | Days | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | 5–7 | The first win of act 1. One small daily action (under 20 min) |
| Monthly | 21–30 | The main challenge of the month |
| Re-entry | 3 | For whoever broke the streak. No guilt: "back in 3 days" |
| Flash | 1–2 | Activity spikes when the feed goes quiet |
| Season | 28–42 | Optional big dated challenge |

- One day = one action, with its time estimate, and the simplest possible
  proof (photo, screenshot, link).
- **One grace day a week plus a 2-minute minimum action** that also saves the
  day. Missing a day does not undo a habit (Lally et al., 2009); feeling that
  everything is lost does. At Duolingo, letting people keep a streak with a
  minimum action raised day-14 retention by 3.3 %, far more than freezing the
  streak (0.38 %).
- **Start on a Monday or the 1st.** Fresh-start dates raise goal pursuit
  (Dai, Milkman & Riis, 2014).
- **The whole ladder should span about 60–70 days.** A habit takes a median of
  66 days to become automatic (range 18–254; Lally et al., 2009). The first 7
  consecutive days matter most: whoever reaches a 7-day streak on Duolingo is
  3.6 times more likely to finish the course.
- **At least 3 exits per challenge**, each with a clear condition: completed →
  next challenge or module; missed 2 days in a row → re-entry; finished with a
  high metric → advanced challenge, audit or guide role.
- The closing always celebrates (wins post, closing call) and **announces what
  comes next**.

## 6. Forum and levels

- 4–7 post categories. Always one for **wins** and one for **questions**; one
  for the engine (daily check-in / ask for your audit / phase deliveries); one
  for announcements (creator only); introductions only if onboarding uses it.
- Name the 9 Skool levels after the journey. A level that unlocks nothing
  does not motivate. Levels 1–3 must be reachable in the first month.

## 7. Weekly rhythm

| Engine | Calls a week | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Challenges | 1 | Week closing: ranking, wins, what comes next |
| Live calls | 2–3 | Live audits, topic of the month, hot seat |
| Program | 1–2 | Delivery review + module questions |

- Same day, same time, every week, created once as a recurring event.
- A call earns its place when it **corrects something concrete**: feedback
  has an effect of d = 0.48 on learning across 435 studies, stronger when it
  says what to change and how (Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie, 2020). The star
  format is the hot seat.
- Don't overload or promise calls that won't happen: 21 % of communities on
  Circle are cutting programming to avoid burnout, and 61 % already use
  asynchronous content as their main channel (Circle, 2025). With less time,
  drop a call and index the recordings by problem.
- Every week: one **wins** post and one post that **announces what's next**.

## 8. Onboarding

- Goal: the first win in 7 days or less.
- Skool allows up to 3 membership questions. Use: starting point (multiple
  choice, so it routes the member), goal for the next 30 days, and **"what day
  and time will you do your first task?"** — stating when and where is what
  most helps people act (implementation intentions: effect d = 0.65 across 94
  studies; Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006). Short forms get answered: 3 questions
  at the door, the long triage after joining.
- Show progress as already started ("You joined", "You answered"): a goal
  that looks begun is finished faster (endowed progress: Nunes & Drèze, 2006;
  goal gradient: Kivetz et al., 2006).
- Messages: on joining (Skool's own AutoDM), day 1, day 3, day 7, and after 5
  days of silence. The day-7 message needs an exit for whoever did nothing.

## Sources

The figures above are the ones cited in the Creator Skool community-building
system: Lally et al. (2009); Dai, Milkman & Riis (2014); Gollwitzer & Sheeran
(2006); Nunes & Drèze (2006); Kivetz et al. (2006); Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente
(*Science*, 2019); Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie (2020); Ruzuku (2026);
RevenueCat (2026); Circle (2025); Duolingo's published streak experiments.
Quote them to a creator as the reason for a rule, never as a promise about
her own community.

## What never to do

- Invent numbers, cases, testimonials, prices, guarantees or urgency. Missing
  → ask, or leave a visible `[placeholder]`.
- Copy another community's sentences. Borrow structure, never wording.
